    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewer Repair in Bossier City

    Clearing a clog can be as little as $300. Structural repairs cost more. Replacing a long run with trenchless methods can run $6,000 or higher, because the price climbs with footage, pipe material, access and whether any excavation is needed at all. You get a firm number after we camera the line.

    Major repairs and replacements in Bossier City, LA usually require one. Small fixes sometimes do not. Rules differ on whether a short spot repair on private property needs a permit, but any work in the public right-of-way or at the connection to the main almost always does. We confirm the requirement before we start.

    Multiple drains backing up at once is the big one. Watch the tub. Water rising in the shower when the washer drains, a sewage smell near the cleanout, and wet or very green patches over the line are all signs the main lateral is blocked, cracked or separated somewhere between the house and the street. We confirm it on camera.

    Fixing the cause, not just the clog. Roots come back if the joint stays open. After a repair, we recommend a camera check in a year or two on older lines, especially if the rest of the run is clay or cast iron that could develop the same problem somewhere else. Watch what goes down the drains.

    Tree roots are the top cause in established neighborhoods. Much of Louisiana sits on soft, expansive clay that swells and shrinks with moisture, which can move slabs and crack buried pipes. Age plays a big part. Cast iron rusts from the inside and clay joints separate, while grease, wipes and paper towels build up on rough or sagging sections until the line closes off. It is usually a combination.

    One slow sink is usually a local clog. Multiple fixtures point to the main. If the toilet bubbles when the tub drains, or the lowest drain in the house backs up whenever someone runs water upstairs, the blockage is almost certainly past the point where the branches join the main line. Time to put a camera in it.

    Flush only toilet paper. Flushable wipes are not. Keep grease out of the kitchen sink, and if your house has mature trees near the line or older clay or cast iron pipe, schedule a camera inspection every year or two so small defects get caught before they turn into a backup. Annual jetting helps on lines with known root trouble.

    A rental auger can open a soft clog. It will not fix a crack. Forcing a cable through a broken or offset joint can snag, tear the pipe further or leave a cutter head stuck underground, which turns a camera visit into an excavation job. Camera first if the clog keeps coming back.
