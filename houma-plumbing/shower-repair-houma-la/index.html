    Showerheads clog from the inside out. Minerals collect behind the face and inside the restrictor, which turns the spray patchy or weak with the valve fully open. Soaking helps sometimes. Otherwise we put on a new head with rubber nozzles that rub clean with your thumb.

    Commercial Shower Service in Houma

    Hotels and dorms cannot wait days on a shower. With commercial cartridges on board, we fix rooms one at a time so the rest stay open. We can work nights. Each repaired valve gets its limit stop set and tested before we move on.

    Dripping Showerhead Repair in Houma

    Drips from the head itself almost always come from the valve, not the head. If the head drips with the valve closed, water is slipping past the cartridge, and a new head will not change that. The fix is in the valve. Drips from the arm threads just need fresh tape and a snug turn.

    Weak Shower Spray Repair in Houma, LA

    Most weak showers come down to the head. Scale packs the nozzles and the restrictor screen behind them, and the spray drops off a little at a time until one day it feels like a garden mister. A soak can bring it back. If the cartridge is clogged too, we replace it and test the flow again.

    Low Shower Pressure Repair in Houma, LA

    Low pressure at the shower with good pressure at the sink narrows it down fast. That points to the head, the flow restrictor or the cartridge, not the main or the water heater, and it is usually something we can fix within an hour. The head comes off first. Low pressure everywhere sends us to the pressure regulator and main valve instead.

    Tub and Shower Combo Repair in Houma, LA

    Tub and shower combos share one valve, one drain and a diverter between them. That means a worn diverter steals pressure from the head, a clogged tub drain slows the shower and a leaky valve shows up at both the spout and the head. Each part gets tested. We also check the overflow gasket, a frequent hidden leak.

    Shower Handle Leak Repair in Houma

    Leaks at the handle or behind the trim plate are easy to miss. Water leaking behind the trim slips into the wall cavity at the valve opening and soaks the drywall while the tile face stays dry. The trim comes off first. It is usually a loose bonnet nut or a worn O-ring.
