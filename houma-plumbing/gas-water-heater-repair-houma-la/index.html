    Repairing Older Gas Water Heaters in Houma

    Older tanks with standing pilots and no vapor sensor are still out there in Houma, mostly in basements and utility closets. Parts for them are simpler, and a universal thermocouple fits most. We can keep a pre-2003 heater running when the tank is sound, but we also check the draft hood, the flue connector and the gas cock, since those fittings on an old install are often original and corroded. Some old setups lack a sediment trap. We add one during the repair.

    Tank Leaks and Pressure Issues in Houma, LA

    Water on the floor near the heater does not always mean the tank is gone. A lot of the time it is the drain valve, the T&P valve or a dripping supply fitting. A relief valve that weeps after every heating cycle usually points to high street pressure or a missing expansion tank, and both are cheaper to fix than a water heater in a Houma, LA home. Water coming from the bottom seam means the tank itself is done. Nothing patches that.

    Pilot Light & Burner Issues in Houma

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. No flame at all usually means a plugged pilot orifice or a bad piezo. Main burner problems show up another way. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. We test each piece in that order.

    Venting and Draft Repair in Houma, LA

    Atmospheric tanks rely on hot exhaust rising through a metal flue with an upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot. A loose, rusted or sagging section lets exhaust spill back into the house. We fix the vent. Each joint gets three screws, the connector gets proper clearance from wood and drywall, and we run the heater for several minutes with a smoke test at the draft hood to watch the exhaust go up. We do not leave with the heater spilling.

    Gas Water Heater Noise Troubleshooting in Houma

    Rumbling or popping from the bottom of the tank is water boiling under a layer of sediment. Hard water and years without a flush both make it worse. We drain the tank through the bottom valve, stir up the settled scale with short bursts of cold water and keep going until it runs clear, which on a neglected tank can take a while. Whistling points somewhere else. A relief valve or a half-closed supply valve is the usual cause.

    Bradford White & Rheem Gas Water Heater Repair in Houma, LA

    Bradford White and Rheem tanks make up a big share of what we repair in Houma, LA. Rheem gas models mostly use White-Rodgers or Honeywell controls with a status light. Bradford White uses its own sealed combustion design with a lint screen at the base and a thermal switch, and it needs a specific kit when the pilot assembly goes bad. We keep both on the truck. Genuine parts keep the warranty intact.

    A weak thermocouple is the usual cause. A dirty pilot orifice can shrink the flame until it no longer heats the thermocouple.     Most repairs take one to three hours. Pilot and thermocouple jobs usually wrap up in under an hour. A gas control valve, a power vent blower or a flue rebuild takes longer, and if the part has to be ordered from a supply house the repair can stretch to a second visit a day or two later.     Sediment is the most common reason. A few inches of scale on the bottom takes up space and insulates the water from the burner.     Lukewarm water, a pilot that keeps going out and a rumbling noise from the bottom of the tank are the usual early signs of trouble. Rusty hot water or a puddle at the base can mean the tank is on its way out. A rotten egg smell is different. Treat it as a gas leak until someone tests it.
