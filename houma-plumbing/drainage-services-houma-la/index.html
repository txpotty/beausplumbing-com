    Frequently Asked Questions About Drainage Services in Houma

    Drain clearing in Houma usually runs $125 to $500 depending on the line, and hydro jetting runs $350 to $800. Yard drains cost more. Most residential French drain or downspout projects land between $2,000 and $8,000, depending on length, depth and how much hardscape we have to cut through or work around.

    Most sink and toilet clogs clear in under an hour. Main lines take longer. With a camera inspection and a clean, accessible cleanout, a main line job usually wraps up in about two hours, though roots or a hard-to-reach cleanout can stretch it to most of a morning.

    Drain cleaning usually means a cable machine with a cutting head that bores through the clog. It works fast and costs less. Jetting scours the full pipe wall, lasting longer, but it costs more and needs sound pipe.

    Call when more than one drain is slow, or when a toilet gurgles while the washer drains. Those point to the main line. In the yard, water that stands for a day or more after rain is the cue.

    Installations like French drains, dry wells and sewer repairs carry a workmanship warranty. Drain clearing has a separate guarantee. The terms are written on your invoice, and if a yard drain we installed does not move water the way we said it would, we come back and correct it at no charge for labor.

    Snaking is the right first step for most single clogs. It is fast and inexpensive. Jetting wins for grease, scale and recurring roots since it cleans the whole wall.

    They solve different problems. French drains handle water moving through soil. A channel drain catches surface water sheeting across concrete, like a driveway that slopes toward the garage, and many yards need one of each because the water arrives both ways during a hard storm.

    Most homes never need scheduled drain cleaning. Clean them when they slow down. Homes with root-prone sewers, old cast iron, or a kitchen that sends a lot of grease down the drain do better with a cleaning every 12 to 24 months, timed before the line slows to a crawl.
