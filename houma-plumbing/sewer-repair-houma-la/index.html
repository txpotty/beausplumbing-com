    A self-leveling camera goes in at the cleanout or through a pulled toilet. We record the whole run.     Cracked and Broken Pipe Repair in Houma

    Clay and cast iron crack from settling soil, tree roots and vehicles parked over the line. The crack lets soil in and sewage out. We excavate at the marked spot, cut out the damaged length and install new Schedule 40 PVC with shielded stainless couplings rated for the transition between materials, then test the repair with water and video before closing the hole. Most of these are one-day jobs.

    Tree Root Invasion Solutions in Houma

    Roots in a sewer line are not the tree's fault. A leaking joint invited them in. Warm, wet, nutrient-rich air escaping from a loose joint draws roots toward the line, and once they get in they keep growing until the joint splits or the pipe cracks open. We cut them out, then talk about the long-term fix. Lining or a joint replacement keeps them from coming back.

    Sewer Line Leak Detection and Repair in Houma, LA

    Sewer smell outside the house points to a leak. So does an extra-green strip of lawn. We confirm it on camera, look for cracks, open joints or a broken cleanout riser, and mark the location so the repair stays small. A small crack can sometimes be fixed with a short sectional liner, so nothing in the yard gets disturbed. Larger breaks get cut out and replaced.

    Orangeburg and Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Houma

    Orangeburg is wood fiber soaked in coal tar. It flattens with age. Cast iron from the same era rusts from the inside, builds scale and eventually splits along the bottom where the sewage runs, and once the camera shows failures along the whole run, patching it stops being worth the money. We replace these lines with PVC. Trenchless bursting is often an option.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewer Repair in Houma

    A single spot repair typically runs $1,500 to $4,500. Depth drives most of that. Trenchless lining usually lands between $80 and $250 per foot, and full replacement of a typical residential lateral ranges from $5,000 to $15,000, with driveways and patios pushing it toward the high end. We price it in writing after the video.

    Clay, cast iron, ABS, PVC and Orangeburg all come up in Houma and around Louisiana. We work on all five. Joining new PVC to old clay or cast iron takes a shielded transition coupling sized to the outside diameter of each pipe, and getting that fitting right is what keeps the repair from leaking at the joint later. We stock the common sizes.

    It depends on the city and the scope. Full replacements almost always do. Small repairs on private property may be exempt, while anything at the street or the public tap typically needs both. We check and pull it if needed.

    A rental auger can open a soft clog. It will not fix a crack. Forcing a cable through a broken or offset joint can snag, tear the pipe further or leave a cutter head stuck underground, which turns a camera visit into an excavation job. Repeat clogs deserve a video inspection.

    Multiple drains backing up at once is the big one. The tub often shows it first. Water rising in the shower when the washer drains, a sewage smell near the cleanout, and wet or very green patches over the line tell you the trouble is in the main line, not one fixture. Video makes it certain.

    Trenchless repair fixes the pipe through one or two small access pits. No long trench. Pipe bursting pulls a new HDPE pipe through the old one while splitting the old pipe apart, and cured-in-place lining inserts a resin-soaked tube that hardens into a new pipe wall inside the old one, which leaves concrete and landscaping alone. Not every line qualifies.

    Fixing the cause, not just the clog. Roots come back if the joint stays open. After a repair, we recommend a camera check in a year or two on older lines, since the same aging pipe elsewhere in the run may start to fail. Keep wipes and grease out of it.

    Our labor warranty is written into the quote. Length depends on the repair. A spot repair with new PVC and a full lining job carry different terms because the materials and risks differ, and we explain exactly what is covered and for how long before the work starts.
