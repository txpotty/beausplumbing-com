    Pedestal Sump Pumps in Houma

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor above the pit, which makes them easier to service and a solid fit for narrow pits in older homes. They typically last a long time because the motor never sits in water. The tradeoff is more noise and a less finished look, so we usually recommend them for utility rooms rather than living spaces. We install them with a rigid discharge, a quality check valve and a float rod set to your pit depth.

    Commercial and Multi-Unit Sump Installations in Houma

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Houma

    The cost for basement sump pump installation in Houma, LA typically ranges from $800 to $2,500, depending on the type of pump, pedestal or submersible, whether a new pit needs to be excavated and whether a battery backup is included. We provide detailed estimates after an on-site assessment.

    Swapping a pump in an existing pit usually takes two to three hours. A new pit with slab cutting and concrete patching usually takes most of a day.

    Manufacturer warranties usually range from 3 to 5 years on quality cast iron pumps. We also back our installation labor, and we will walk you through the specific coverage for the model you choose.

    We routinely install Zoeller, Liberty Pumps and Wayne because they offer cast iron housings, reliable switches and good parts support. We will match the brand and model to your pit size and budget.

    In many areas, including Houma, a plumbing permit may be required for new sump pump installations or major replacements to confirm compliance with local building codes. Beau's Plumbing will help you work through the permit process and make sure all work is up to code.

    Beau's Plumbing offers a warranty on our labor for basement sump pump installations, typically for one year. The sump pump itself comes with a manufacturer's warranty, which can range from 1 to 5 years depending on the brand and model. We will provide you all warranty details at the time of installation.

    Replacing a pump usually does not need a permit, but new pits, new discharge routes or new electrical circuits may. We check local requirements and pull permits when they are needed.

    Common signs you might need a new sump pump include unusual noises during operation, visible rust or corrosion on the unit, frequent cycling on and off, or a pump that fails to turn on during heavy rain.
