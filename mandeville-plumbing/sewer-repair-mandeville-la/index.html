    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewer Repair in Mandeville

    A single spot repair typically runs $1,500 to $4,500. Depth drives most of that. Trenchless lining usually lands between $80 and $250 per foot, and full replacement of a typical residential lateral ranges from $5,000 to $15,000, depending on length and what sits on top of the pipe. We price it in writing after the video.

    One slow sink is usually a local clog. Multiple fixtures point to the main. If the toilet bubbles when the tub drains, or the lowest drain in the house backs up whenever someone runs water upstairs, you are looking at a problem in the main line downstream of every branch. Time to put a camera in it.

    Most spot repairs are done in one day, excavation and backfill included. Some take longer. Larger replacements, or jobs that need a right-of-way permit to dig near the street, can take two to four days depending on the inspection schedule and the length of pipe. We put the schedule in writing.

    Clay, cast iron, ABS, PVC and Orangeburg all come up in Mandeville and around Louisiana. We repair every one of them. Joining new PVC to old clay or cast iron takes a shielded transition coupling sized to the outside diameter of each pipe, and getting that fitting right is what keeps the repair from leaking at the joint later. The usual couplings ride on the truck.

    Watch for clogs that keep coming back in more than one drain. Gurgling toilets are another clue. A sewer smell in the yard, a strip of grass that is greener than the rest, or water rising in the shower when the washing machine empties all point to a problem in the main line rather than a single fixture. A camera inspection settles it.

    A rental auger can open a soft clog. It will not fix a crack. Forcing a cable through a broken or offset joint can snag, tear the pipe further or leave a cutter head stuck underground, which turns a camera visit into an excavation job. Camera first if the clog keeps coming back.

    Tree roots are the top cause in established neighborhoods. Much of Louisiana sits on soft, expansive clay that swells and shrinks with moisture, which can move slabs and crack buried pipes. Age plays a big part. Cast iron rusts from the inside and clay joints separate, while grease, wipes and paper towels catch on those rough spots until nothing gets through. It is usually a combination.

    Multiple drains backing up at once is the big one. Watch the tub.
