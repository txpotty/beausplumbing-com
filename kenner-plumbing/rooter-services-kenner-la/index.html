    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Kenner

    Rooting a sink, tub or toilet typically runs $125 to $300. Main sewer rooting through a cleanout generally runs $200 to $500. Adding a jetting pass brings the total to about $450 to $1,000, depending on line length and how much grease is in it. Nights and weekends run higher.

    Our trucks carry Spartan and RIDGID machines, plus jetters sized for residential and commercial lines. They hold up well to heavy daily use. We keep cables and cutter heads in several sizes on board, which lets the tech match the tool to your pipe instead of forcing one setup on every drain.

    Snaking runs a spinning cable with a cutting head through the clog. Hydro jetting sends water at several thousand psi through a nozzle that scrubs the whole pipe wall as it is pulled back toward the cleanout. Jetting cleans more. We usually jet recurring grease lines and scaled mains, and snake the one-off clogs.

    Rooting uses a spinning cable with cutting blades to break up clogs and roots. Jetting is different. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scrub the pipe walls clean. Tough lines often get both, rooting to open the path and jetting to clean out what remains.

    Sewer pipes leak a little water vapor at the joints. Roots follow that moisture to the gap and push in. Once in, they grow quickly. In Kenner, clay with joints every couple of feet and old cast iron with lead and oakum joints get hit the most.

    Rental machines are easy to get, but they are dangerous in untrained hands. A cable can kink and wrap a hand or wrist fast. They also get stuck. A rental cable stuck in an old clay line can turn a $300 rooter call into a dig to retrieve it.

    Chemicals sometimes open a small hole in a hair clog, but they will not touch roots or a grease plug. Repeated use can weaken old PVC joints and pit cast iron. They are dangerous to handle. A rooter cable removes the clog itself rather than eating a channel through it, so the line stays open far longer.

    Rooting is safe with a trained tech and the correct cable and blade. Badly cracked or collapsed pipe can be harmed by any cleaning method. Feel matters. If the cable tells us the pipe is damaged, we stop and send in a camera before going further.
