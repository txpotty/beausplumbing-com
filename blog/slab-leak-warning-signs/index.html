    Slab Leak Warning Signs Every Louisiana Homeowner Should Know

    By Aaron Mitchell · October 15, 2026 · 5 min read

    What a Slab Leak Is

    A slab leak is a break in a water or drain line that runs under or through the concrete floor of your house. Nobody sees it at first. Most supply lines under a slab are soft copper or PEX, and the trouble usually starts as a pinhole no bigger than a pencil tip. Ground movement and temperature swings work on that pipe for decades.

    Long, hot summers and heavy humidity are the main stress on plumbing in Louisiana, driving condensation, mold, and overworked water heaters.

    Concrete hides the damage, so a leak can run for months before anyone notices. By then the water has usually soaked the pad under the flooring. We have pulled up laminate where the slab underneath was wet from one wall to the other, and the only clue the owner had was a water bill that doubled. Early means a smaller hole in the floor.

    Homes are commonly built on slabs or raised pier foundations, and raised homes keep much of the plumbing easier to reach.

    Soil under a house expands when wet and shrinks when dry, and every cycle puts a little more load on pipe that is locked in concrete. Copper thins out wherever it rubs on gravel or a piece of rebar. Hard water speeds that wear from the inside. PVC drain lines under the slab crack at the joints more often than along the pipe.

    Signs You Can Check Yourself

    A Water Bill That Jumps for No Reason

    Pull up the last six months of bills and compare the gallons, not the dollars. A jump of 20 or 30 percent with no new pool or sprinkler schedule deserves a look. Then do the meter test. Turn off every faucet and appliance, note the reading, and leave the water untouched for two hours. A meter that moved means water is leaving the system somewhere between the meter and your fixtures, and under the slab is a common spot.

    Warm Spots on the Floor

    A hot water line leaking under tile will heat the slab above it. Try walking the floors barefoot early in the day. What you want to find is one warm patch while the rest of the tile is cool, usually close to a bathroom or the kitchen. Pets find these spots before people do, so watch where the dog likes to lie in winter.

    Running Water With Everything Off

    Stand in a quiet house at night and listen near the floor. A hiss or a faint rush with no fixture running points to pressure escaping from a supply line. Some leaks are loud enough to hear. Others need an acoustic listening device, which is what our techs use to pinpoint the spot within a foot or two.

    New Cracks in Walls or Flooring

    Water under a slab washes out fine soil and lets the concrete settle unevenly. The usual clue is a crack in the tile or concrete running straight across a room. Doors that suddenly stick are another clue. Drywall cracks that start at the corner of a door frame and angle upward tell us the slab moved, and a leak is one of the reasons it does.

    Musty Smell or Mildew at the Baseboards

    Carpet over a leak stays damp and starts to smell like a basement. Look for dark staining along the baseboard or tack strip. Mildew on the bottom few inches of drywall, with no plumbing fixture on the other side of the wall, points to moisture wicking up from below. A moisture meter confirms it in seconds.

    Getting a Slab Leak Found and Fixed

    Do not start breaking concrete on a guess. A proper leak locate uses pressure testing on each line, then acoustic and thermal tools to find the exact spot. From there the choice is usually between a spot repair through the slab and a reroute through the attic or walls. The right choice depends mostly on pipe age and how many leaks the house has already had.
