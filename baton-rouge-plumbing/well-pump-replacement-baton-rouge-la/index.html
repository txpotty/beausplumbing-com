    Sandy Wells and Submersible Pumps in Baton Rouge

    Sand kills submersibles early. A well that pumps sand will wear impellers down in a few years, so we check for grit in the old pump and talk about setting the new one higher off the bottom or adding a sand separator. Where the pump hangs matters.

    Well Pump Electrical Troubleshooting in Baton Rouge, LA

    No-water calls often end at the control box, not the pump. Three-wire pumps have a control box with a start capacitor and relay, and those parts fail far more often than the motor itself. Those get tested first. Swapping a capacitor costs a fraction of pulling a pump.

    Grundfos Well Pump Replacement in Baton Rouge, LA

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have a built-in soft start and dry-run protection, and they run on two wires with no control box at the surface. They cost more up front. Add the CU 301 controller to an SQE and you get constant pressure.

    What a Well Pump Job Costs in Baton Rouge, LA

    A well pump job usually runs a few thousand dollars, and the pump itself is often less than half of that. The rest is labor, wire, pipe and fittings, plus the hoist time to get it all down the hole. You see every line item.

    Franklin Electric Well Pump Replacement in Baton Rouge

    Franklin Electric builds a large share of the submersible motors sold in the US, including ones sitting under other brands' pump ends. Replacement control boxes are easy to source. We match the motor to the pump end and the wire size to the run length.

    Constant Pressure Pump Systems in Baton Rouge

    Franklin SubDrive and Grundfos SQE are the two constant pressure systems we install most. Both use a small 2 to 20 gallon tank instead of a big one, since the drive keeps the pump running at a matched speed. The soft start is easier on the motor. The upfront cost is higher.

    Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Baton Rouge

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes a healthy pump look sick. Short cycling, where the pump starts every few seconds of water use, usually means the tank bladder has failed and the pump is paying for it. A tire gauge on the air valve tells us a lot. The air charge should read 2 psi under the switch cut-in.
