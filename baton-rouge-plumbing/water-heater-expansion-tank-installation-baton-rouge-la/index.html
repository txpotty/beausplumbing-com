    Yes, expansion tanks need occasional maintenance to keep working correctly. It is recommended to have the air pressure checked annually to match the incoming water pressure. This preventive check helps extend the life of the tank and your water heater.

    Most installations take one to two hours. Attic installs or PRV replacements can add an hour or two.

    When water heats up, it expands. Without an expansion tank, this expanded water has nowhere to go, increasing pressure within your closed plumbing system. This constant pressure can prematurely wear out water heater components and relief valves and even cause leaks or ruptures. The expansion tank absorbs this excess volume, protecting your entire system.

    A standalone expansion tank generally does not require a permit, but one is often included when it is installed with a new water heater. We follow local requirements on every job.

    Signs of a failing expansion tank include water dripping from the tank's air valve, rust or corrosion on the tank's exterior or a water heater that frequently cycles on and off. You might also notice fluctuating water pressure or a banging sound in your pipes, which points to excessive pressure buildup.
