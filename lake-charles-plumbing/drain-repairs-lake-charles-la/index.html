    Frequently Asked Questions About Drain Repairs in Lake Charles

    A simple clog clearing starts around $150. Bigger repairs climb fast. Complex main line repairs can run past $3,000, depending on the drain involved, the extent of the damage and the access. We price it after we look.

    Snugging a slip nut or replacing a plastic trap is a fair DIY job. Hand-tight plus a quarter turn. If the leak comes from a cracked tailpiece, a corroded metal drain or somewhere inside the wall, it needs a plumber who can fix the pipe and look for rot nearby. Cabinet rot hides well.

    Repeat clogs come from something in the pipe catching debris. Usually it is buildup. Poor pipe slope, a missing vent or a cracked fitting with a rough edge catches debris over and over, even after the line is cleared. A camera shows which one you have.

    Cleaning removes a blockage from a sound pipe. Repair changes the pipe itself. A crack, a sag, a broken fitting or a missing vent will keep causing clogs no matter how often the line gets cleaned, so a drain that clogs repeatedly usually needs repair rather than more snaking. We check on video.

    Tightening a slip nut or replacing a plastic trap is fair game. Hand snakes are fine too. Anything inside a wall, under a floor or involving cast iron, or a leak that keeps returning after you have tightened it, is a job for a licensed plumber with the right tools. Skip the caustic cleaners.

    For a fixture unclog or a trap swap, usually not. Major work often needs one. Replacing drain lines, especially work involving excavation or the main sewer line, often needs a plumbing permit from Lake Charles, which lets an inspector look at the pipe before it gets covered. We handle the paperwork.

    A trap swap or a fixture drain fix is usually an hour or two. Bigger jobs run past a day. Replacing a section of main line can take a full day or several days, with permits and digging as the main things that stretch it out. We give you the timeline up front.

    Age and corrosion lead the list. Soil movement is close behind. Tree roots, temperature swings, heavy loads over buried pipe, and poor original work like unprimed glue joints or missing support straps all cause early failure. It is usually a combination.
