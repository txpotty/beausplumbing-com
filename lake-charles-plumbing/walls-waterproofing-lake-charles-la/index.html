    Waterproofing for Older Homes in Lake Charles, LA

    Houses built before modern drainage rules often have no footing drain at all, or a clay tile drain that collapsed decades ago. Newer construction mostly uses PEX and plastic drains, while older homes often mix copper, galvanized, and cast iron. Fieldstone and old brick foundations need breathable products. Sealing soft old brick with a rigid coating traps water inside it and makes the face spall off over a few seasons, so we use lime-compatible or vapor-open coatings and pair them with a new interior drain. We repoint failed mortar joints before sealing.

    Laundry Room and Utility Wall Waterproofing in Lake Charles, LA

    Rubber washing machine hoses burst. We swap them for braided stainless hoses, put a drain pan under the washer with a line to a floor drain where one is available, and install a quarter-turn shutoff or an automatic leak valve on the supply box.     Foundation Crack Repair & Sealing in Lake Charles

    Hairline cracks in poured concrete walls leak more water than their size suggests. We inject them with low-pressure polyurethane, which expands to fill the crack from the inside face to the soil side, or with epoxy when the crack also needs structural bonding. Horizontal cracks are different. A horizontal crack often signals soil pressure pushing the wall in, so we check for movement with a level before we seal anything. Vertical cracks are usually a one-visit job.

    Crawl Space Encapsulation in Lake Charles

    Encapsulation seals the whole crawl space, walls and floor, with a reinforced poly liner. We run 12 to 20 mil liner up the walls, tape every seam, seal around piers and pipe penetrations, and usually add a dehumidifier to hold humidity under 60 percent. Humid air causes sweating pipes and toilet tanks, and that moisture can damage subfloors and framing if leaks go unnoticed. Wood rot slows to a stop. Drier air under the floor also means less musty air upstairs.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Lake Charles

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most dependable way to stop a basement leak, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing to carry the water away from the wall. Low-lying areas have a high water table and settling ground, which can cause sewer lines to sag, crack, or separate over time. Backfill goes in last.
