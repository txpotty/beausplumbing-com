    What Our Water Line Service Covers in Lafayette, LA

    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Lafayette

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Louisiana has many older homes with cast iron drains and galvanized steel supply pipes that corrode faster in warm, wet conditions. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. We pinpoint the leak with acoustic gear and thermal imaging. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or abandon the old pipe under the floor and run new PEX above it. You get a price on every option.

    Main Water Line Leak Repair in Lafayette

    A meter that moves with every fixture off is the clearest sign you have. We confirm it in about ten minutes. The house shutoff gets closed, the dial gets watched, and if it keeps creeping we know the leak is between the meter and the house. Ground microphones find the spot. A repair coupling or a new length of pipe goes in, and then we pressure test it.

    Water Pressure Restoration in Lafayette

    Low pressure at every fixture points to the service line or the regulator, not the faucets. We put a gauge on a hose bib first. Then we check the pressure reducing valve, the house shutoff and the meter-side valve for a partly closed gate, and look at the pipe itself. Old galvanized can be choked to half its size with rust. The fix might be a new valve or a new line.

    Emergency Water Line Break Repair in Lafayette, LA

    A full break can flood the yard and leave the house dry at the same time. We shut it down first. The crew pumps the hole, finds the break and decides whether a coupling will do or whether a section needs replacing, and if the repair will run past a few hours we set a temporary bypass to the house. Most breaks are fixed the same day. We photograph the damage for your insurance or HOA.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Repair in Lafayette

    High static pressure shortens the life of the service line and every fixture behind it. Most codes cap it at 80 psi. We gauge the pressure at a hose bib, and if it reads high we replace the pressure reducing valve with a Watts or Zurn Wilkins unit. Old gate valves that will not close get replaced with full-port ball valves. That way you can actually shut the water off.

    Repair vs. Replace Your Water Line in Lafayette, LA

    One leak on a newer copper or PEX line is a spot repair. Repeated leaks on galvanized pipe call for a new line. Age matters too, since most service lines past 40 years old are living on borrowed time. Spot repairs often run a few hundred dollars while a full replacement costs more and resets the clock for decades. We show you the pipe we exposed.

    Galvanized Pipe Water Line Repair in Lafayette, LA

    Galvanized steel service lines rust from the inside out. Once one spot leaks, the pipe on either side is usually just as thin. We can clamp or replace a short section to get you through, which can hold for a while if the pipe is not too far gone. More often we recommend replacing the whole run from the meter to the house with copper or PEX. That fixes the pressure too.

    Underground Water Line Leak Repair in Lafayette

    Underground leaks hide well. Many people find out from the bill before they see anything in the yard. Others notice a patch of grass that grows greener than the rest, or a spot near the walk that stays muddy days after rain. We confirm the leak at the meter, trace the pipe route and listen along it. We dig where the noise is loudest.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Lafayette

    A minor leak repair often starts around $500. Replacing the whole line is a bigger number. A meter-to-house replacement or a trenchless pull can reach $5,000 or more on a long run or a deep line, and the price moves with pipe type, depth and whether we trench or bore. We quote it in writing after the inspection.

    Often the pipe itself is not covered. Resulting damage sometimes is. Some homeowners carry separate service line coverage through their insurer or the water provider, and that coverage usually pays for the line repair. We provide photos and an itemized invoice for your claim.

    A typical spot repair runs two to four hours, from locating the leak to filling the hole. Replacements take longer. Deep lines, long runs and jobs under concrete or pavers can push the job into a second or third day. You get a realistic timeline when we hand you the quote.

    We dig a pit at each end of the old line. A cable goes through the old pipe. A bursting head on the cable splits the old line outward, while the new pipe is pulled into the path it just opened. It only works when the old pipe is a fairly straight run, and most home service lines qualify.

    Two to four hours for most spot repairs. A full replacement is typically a one-day job. Trenchless jobs are often a single day too, though digging the two pits and setting up the boring equipment takes a few hours on its own before any pipe moves. In most cases your water is back on before evening.

    Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. A hiss at the meter is a giveaway. Low pressure at every fixture, rusty water and a climbing bill are other common clues. The surest check is your meter. A moving dial means water is escaping somewhere.

    We work on copper, PEX, HDPE poly, PVC and old galvanized steel. We also run into gray polybutylene now and then. For new work in Lafayette, we usually recommend Type K copper or PEX rated for direct burial, or HDPE for a trenchless pull. The trucks carry transition fittings for every one of them.

    Type K copper, burial-rated PEX or HDPE poly. Each has its place. Copper resists punctures and lasts decades, PEX flexes with the soil and HDPE is what we use for trenchless pulls. We recommend one after looking at your soil, line length and budget.
