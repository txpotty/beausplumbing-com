    Jet Pump Seal and Motor Replacement in Lafayette, LA

    Single-stage and two-stage jet pumps wear out at the seal and impeller, and a leaking shaft seal shows up as a puddle under the motor. New pump, primed and tested. After that, we watch the pressure gauge cycle from cut-in to cut-out.

    Jet Pump Replacement in Lafayette, LA

    Jet pumps sit above ground, in a well house or basement, and pull water up by suction from wells where the water level is within about 25 feet. Deep-well jets with a two-pipe setup can reach further, but they work harder. Lost prime is the classic complaint. Most of the time a bad foot valve or a suction leak is letting water drain back, and we check those before condemning the pump.

    Well Pump Sizing in Lafayette

    Sizing starts with the well log. The log, or a sounder down the casing, gives us static water level and well depth, and a draw-down test tells us roughly how many gallons per minute the well can actually give. Pump more than the well yields and it runs dry.

    Well Pump Brands We Install in Lafayette, LA

    Goulds, Grundfos, Franklin Electric and Sta-Rite cover nearly every well pump we install. We pick by the well, not the logo. Parts for Goulds and Franklin are easy to get, which matters when a control box dies on a weekend.

    Franklin Electric Well Pump Replacement in Lafayette

    Franklin Electric builds a large share of the submersible motors sold in the US, including ones sitting under other brands' pump ends. Parts and control boxes are easy to find. We match the motor to the pump end and the wire size to the run length.

    Drop Pipe and Wire Replacement in Lafayette

    Galvanized drop pipe from the 1970s and 1980s rusts from the inside, and a pinhole at 150 feet will bleed pressure every time the pump shuts off. Depending on depth and pump weight, it gets replaced with schedule 120 PVC or poly. The wire gets checked too. Cracked insulation and taped splices get cut out.

    Constant Pressure Pump Systems in Lafayette

    Franklin SubDrive and Grundfos SQE are the two constant pressure systems we install most. They get by with a much smaller tank because the drive matches pump speed to demand. Soft starts also cut wear on the motor. The upfront cost is higher.
