    What Sewer Replacement Costs in Lafayette, LA

    Pipe Bursting Under Driveways in Lafayette, LA

    Concrete is expensive to replace. With pipe bursting, the new line goes in under the driveway or tree from one pit to another, following the old pipe's route. The HDPE is butt-fused on site into one long piece. A typical burst takes one working day. You end up patching two small pits rather than a whole slab.

    Clay and Orangeburg Sewer Replacement in Lafayette, LA

    Orangeburg is tar-soaked wood fiber. It was installed in a lot of homes in the decades after the war, and over time it softens, blisters and squashes into an oval that catches everything going by. Clay is tougher but leaks roots. Several bad spots on one run usually make full replacement the better buy.

    Replacing a Collapsed Sewer by Trench in Lafayette

    Collapsed pipe cannot be burst or lined. When the camera stops at a pile of broken clay or a flattened section of Orangeburg, the only fix is to dig down, clear the debris and lay new pipe through that stretch. Being in the trench shows us what the surrounding soil is doing. Sometimes we find a washout or a root mat that explains why the pipe failed in the first place.

    Cured-in-Place Sewer Lining in Lafayette

    Lining builds a new pipe inside the old one. After cutting roots and jetting the line, we pull in a resin-filled liner and inflate it until it cures against the old pipe wall. Collapsed pipe will not work. It is a strong choice under slabs and finished rooms. You will get a straight answer on whether yours is a candidate.

    How Pipe Bursting Works in Lafayette

    A winch pulls the bursting head. The head is slightly bigger than the old pipe, so it splits clay or cast iron outward into the soil while fused HDPE trails behind it in one continuous piece. You get a pit at each end. With no joints in the new pipe, roots have no way back in.
