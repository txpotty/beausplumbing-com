    From the Meter to the Faucet: Leak Detection in New Orleans, LA

    Water Meter Leak Testing in New Orleans

    The meter is the first and simplest leak test. Everything off, then watch. If the leak indicator spins with every fixture and appliance off, water is escaping somewhere, and the flow rate gives a rough count of gallons lost per day. Then we shut off zones one at a time.

    Pool Water Leak Detection in New Orleans, LA

    Summer heat and wind pull a lot of water from a pool, which lets a leak blend in with evaporation. A bucket test separates the two. Then we pressure test the suction and return lines and dye test around lights, skimmers and fittings, since a running pool leak can wash out the soil behind the shell.

    Commercial Water Leak Detection in New Orleans, LA

    Offices, shops and apartment buildings in New Orleans run long supply lines where leaks can hide. Downtime costs money. Beau's Plumbing isolates each zone and tests risers, mains and branch lines with pressure gauges, then locates leaks with acoustic and thermal tools, often after hours so tenants and customers are not disturbed.

    Pinhole Leak Detection in New Orleans, LA

    Pinholes start as a mist and grow into a spray. They show up most in copper. Our techs use moisture meters to find damp framing and thermal cameras to spot cool wet areas, tracing the leak without opening every wall in the house. Fast repairs prevent mold. Humid air causes sweating pipes and toilet tanks, and that moisture can rot subfloors and framing if leaks go unnoticed.

    Smart Leak Detection Devices in New Orleans

    Smart shutoff valves like Moen Flo and Phyn monitor flow and shut the water off automatically when they see a leak pattern. Sensors go by water heaters and washers. Our plumbers handle install and setup so alerts come to your phone, and some insurers give a premium discount for having one.

    Irrigation Leak Detection in New Orleans

    Drip and sprinkler lines cover the yard underground, and they hide a lot of water loss. Stuck valves are common. We isolate the irrigation system from the house, run each zone and trace leaking lines, since a cracked lateral or broken emitter can lose thousands of gallons unnoticed.

    Water Heater and Softener Leaks in New Orleans

    Softeners stuck in regeneration can drain water around the clock. That shows up on the bill. Water heaters leak at fittings, drain valves and relief valves, and a dripping relief valve often means high pressure or a failed expansion tank. We check both units on every visit.

    Main Service Line Leaks in New Orleans, LA

    The service line carries full street pressure and may leak below ground for months. Soft spots in the lawn may be the only sign. We trace the line, listen for the leak and mark it for a targeted repair, usually suggesting replacement when the line is old galvanized or polybutylene.
