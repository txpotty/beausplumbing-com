    Basement Wall Sealing in Metairie, LA

    Concrete block is porous, and its hollow cores fill with water like a sponge. Long, hot summers and heavy humidity are the main stress on plumbing in Louisiana, driving condensation, mold, and hard-working water heaters.     Shower Pan Leak Repair and Waterproofing in Metairie, LA

    Wet baseboards outside the shower or a stain on the ceiling below usually point to the pan. We plug the drain, fill the pan with an inch or two of water, and mark the waterline and check it a day later. A drop means a leak. Rebuilding means a mortar pre-slope of a quarter inch per foot, a new PVC liner or bonded membrane, and a clamping drain tightened onto the liner. We flood test the new pan before any tile goes down.

    Foundation Crack Repair & Sealing in Metairie

    Hairline cracks in poured concrete walls leak more water than their size suggests. We inject them with low-pressure polyurethane foam, which expands to fill the crack from the inside face to the soil side, or with epoxy when the crack also needs structural bonding. Horizontal cracks are different. A horizontal crack often signals soil pressure pushing the wall in, so we check for movement with a level before we seal anything. Vertical cracks are usually a one-visit job.

    Interior Walls Waterproofing in Metairie, LA

    Interior drain tile handles water that already got through the wall. We cut a channel in the slab along the perimeter, lay perforated pipe in washed stone, and tie it into a sump pit so the pump can push the water outside. The wall still gets wet. The difference is that the water no longer ends up on your floor. This route costs less than digging outside and makes sense when a patio or driveway sits against the foundation.

    Waterproofing for Older Homes in Metairie, LA

    Houses built before modern drainage rules often have no footing drain at all, or a clay tile drain that collapsed decades ago. Louisiana has many older homes with cast iron drains and galvanized supply pipes that corrode faster in warm, wet conditions. Fieldstone and old brick foundations need breathable products. A rigid coating on soft brick traps moisture and makes the face spall off over a few seasons, so we use lime-compatible or vapor-open coatings and pair them with interior drain tile. Old mortar joints get repointed first.
