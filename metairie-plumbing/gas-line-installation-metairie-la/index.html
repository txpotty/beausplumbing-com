    Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Line Installation in Metairie

    A simple indoor gas line for a range or dryer usually costs $350 to $1,000. Outdoor runs to a grill, fire pit or pool heater usually range from $1,000 to $3,500 depending on distance, trenching and line size.

    Gas lines usually do not need frequent inspections once installed correctly, but it is good practice to have them checked during routine plumbing inspections or if you notice unusual smells or sounds. Homes from the late seventies through the mid nineties may contain polybutylene supply pipe, which is prone to failure. For older homes in Metairie, an inspection every 5 to 10 years can help catch potential issues.

    The most critical sign of a gas line issue is the smell of rotten eggs, which signals a gas leak. Other signs include a hissing sound near a gas line, visible damage to a pipe or dead vegetation over an underground gas line. If you suspect a gas leak, evacuate immediately and call your gas provider and then us.

    We use schedule 40 black iron, CSST flexible tubing and yellow polyethylene for underground runs. The choice is based on location, code requirements and the appliance.

    We use proven, industry-standard brands for gas line materials and fittings for durability and safety. This includes manufacturers like Gastite and OmegaFlex for CSST, and high-quality black iron pipe and fittings from reputable suppliers. All materials are chosen to meet or exceed local plumbing codes.

    Gas line installation is highly dangerous and should only be performed by licensed professionals. Improper installation can lead to gas leaks, explosions, fires and carbon monoxide poisoning. It also voids warranties and often fails local inspections, so it is not a DIY project.

    A rotten egg or sulfur smell, a hissing sound near a line or appliance, and dead vegetation over a buried line are red flags. If you smell gas, leave the home and call your gas utility right away.
