    What Tankless Maintenance Saves in Alexandria

    Scale on an exchanger acts like insulation. A heavily scaled unit burns more gas per shower than a clean one, and that adds up each month. We lay out the maintenance price next to the likely repair cost so you can decide.

    Flush Now or Replace the Exchanger Later in Alexandria, LA

    A new heat exchanger can run $600 to $1,500 or more installed, and some manufacturers will not cover one that failed from scale. Descaling costs far less. Cared for, many units run 15 to 20 years. We will also say whether a softener or a scale inhibitor makes sense for your water.

    Tankless Vent Inspection in Alexandria

    Gas tankless heaters push exhaust out through a vent that has to stay sealed from the heater all the way to the termination outside the house. Condensing units use PVC or polypropylene vent pipe, pitched back toward the heater so condensate drains. We check every joint and test for carbon monoxide near the unit. Wasp nests in vent caps are common.

    Tankless Banks for Restaurants and Apartments in Alexandria

    Cascade systems link several tankless units to one controller that fires them in turn. They see heavy use, and scale builds faster than at home. We descale each heater in the bank, check the cascade controller and confirm the load is shared evenly across every unit. Quarterly service is common.

    Home Tankless Flushing in Alexandria

    Private wells serve many rural homes, and sediment, iron, and bacteria make periodic well testing a good habit. Inside a tankless heater, that water runs through a small copper or stainless coil while the burner fires, and every minute of hot water leaves a little calcium behind. We hook a pump to the service valves and circulate descaling solution for about an hour. The flow returns. The temperature holds steady and the high-limit shutdowns stop.

    Step-by-Step Tankless Flushing in Alexandria

    First we shut off the gas and power, then close both isolation valves so the heater is cut off from the house plumbing. The pump pulls solution from a bucket, through the cold port, out the hot port and back again. Once the cycle ends, we rinse with clean water and pull the inlet screen. Then we check for leaks.
