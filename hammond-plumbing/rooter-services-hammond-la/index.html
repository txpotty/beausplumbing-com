    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Hammond

    Rooting a sink, tub or toilet typically runs $125 to $300. Main sewer rooting through a cleanout generally runs $200 to $500. With hydro jetting added, expect roughly $450 to $1,000, depending on how long the line is and how heavy the grease is. After-hours calls cost more.

    We run RIDGID, Spartan and Gorlitz augers, plus sewer cameras and jetters from the same tier of commercial makers. They survive heavy daily use. Brand matters less than setup. A good machine with the wrong cutter can still damage a pipe, so we spend as much care picking the head as we do the machine.

    Snaking runs a spinning cable with a cutting head through the clog. Hydro jetting sends water at several thousand psi through a nozzle that scrubs the whole pipe wall as it is pulled back toward the cleanout. Jetting cleans more. We usually jet recurring grease lines and scaled mains, and snake the one-off clogs.

    Rooting is safe with a trained tech and the correct cable and blade. Collapsed or badly cracked pipe can be hurt by anything run through it. The tech's touch matters. If the cable tells us the pipe is damaged, we stop and send in a camera before going further.

    Chemicals sometimes open a small hole in a hair clog, but they will not touch roots or a grease plug. Repeated use can weaken old PVC joints and pit cast iron. They are dangerous to handle. A rooter cable removes the clog itself rather than eating a channel through it, so the line stays open far longer.

    Keep grease, coffee grounds and wipes out of drains. Hair strainers in tubs and showers help. After using the disposal, run hot water for a bit and scrape food into the trash first to keep the kitchen line clearer. Older lines with roots need scheduled rooting every 12 to 24 months.

    Our trucks carry Spartan and RIDGID machines, plus jetters sized for residential and commercial lines. Both brands hold up to daily use. Several sizes of cable and cutter ride on each truck, so the tech matches the tool to the pipe rather than using one setup everywhere.

    Rental machines are easy to get, but they are dangerous in untrained hands. A cable can kink and wrap a hand or wrist fast. They also get stuck. Getting a rental cable stuck in clay pipe can mean digging it out, which costs far more than a rooter visit.
