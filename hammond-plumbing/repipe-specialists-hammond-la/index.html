    Most PEX repipes for Louisiana homes cost $5,000 to $12,000, depending on size, bathrooms and access. Copper repipes usually run 20 to 40 percent more.

    While fixing individual leaks might seem cheaper in the short term, it's often a temporary solution for an aging plumbing system. If you're experiencing multiple leaks, low pressure, or discolored water, it indicates widespread pipe wear. Repiping addresses the root cause by replacing the entire system, providing a long-term, dependable solution and preventing future problems.

    Some Louisiana groundwater carries iron and sulfur, which can cause sulfur smells, stains, and buildup in water heaters. Hard water can lead to mineral buildup, or scale, inside pipes over time. This buildup restricts water flow, reduces pressure, and can speed up corrosion, especially in older metal pipes. Repiping with materials like PEX or properly installed copper, along with considering a water softener, can reduce these effects and extend the life of your plumbing system.

    Most homes are repiped in two to four days, plus a day for drywall patching. Water is usually restored each evening.

    Common signs you need repiping include persistent low water pressure throughout your home, discolored or rusty-looking water, frequent pipe leaks, visible corrosion on exposed pipes, and a metallic taste or odor in your water. If your home is over 50 years old with original pipes, it's a good idea to have them inspected, especially if they are galvanized or polybutylene.
